educationSections:
  - heading: Why Homeowners Consider Metal Roofing
    intro: >-
      Metal isn't the right fit for every home or every budget, but it's
      worth understanding what it offers before ruling it out.
    items:
      - title: Long service-life potential
        description: >-
          Metal roofing systems are generally built to last longer than a
          standard asphalt shingle roof, when installed correctly and
          maintained.
      - title: Weather resistance
        description: >-
          A properly installed metal roof sheds rain efficiently and holds
          up well to the wind and wet conditions common in Western
          Washington.
      - title: Lower routine maintenance
        description: >-
          Metal roofs don't grow moss the way shingles can, which can mean
          less routine upkeep over time, though gutters and flashing still
          need attention.
      - title: Clean architectural appearance
        description: >-
          Standing-seam and panel profiles give a home a distinct, modern
          look that some homeowners specifically want.
      - title: Snow and rain shedding
        description: >-
          The smooth surface and steeper effective pitch of many metal
          systems help snow and heavy rain shed off the roof quickly.
      - title: Higher initial investment
        description: >-
          Metal roofing typically costs more upfront than asphalt shingles.
          Homeowners weigh that against the potential for a longer service
          life before deciding.

  - heading: Metal Roof vs. Asphalt Shingles
    intro: >-
      Neither material is universally better. The right choice depends on
      the home, the budget, and what the homeowner is optimizing for.
    items:
      - title: Initial investment
        description: Asphalt shingles cost less upfront. Metal costs more upfront but is often positioned as a longer-term investment.
      - title: Appearance
        description: Shingles offer a traditional look in many colors and textures. Metal offers a cleaner, more modern profile.
      - title: Maintenance
        description: Shingles can develop moss and granule loss over time. Metal generally needs less routine attention to the roofing surface itself.
      - title: Repairability
        description: Damaged shingles are usually straightforward to replace individually. Metal panel repairs typically require a specialist familiar with the system.
      - title: Installation
        description: Shingle installation is common and widely understood. Metal installation requires specific technique and experience to get flashing and seams right.
      - title: Homeowner goals
        description: Homeowners planning to sell soon may prioritize upfront cost. Homeowners planning to stay long-term may weigh service life more heavily.

  - heading: When Does A Metal Roof Make Sense?
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A metal roof tends to make the most sense for homeowners planning to
        stay in their home for a long time, who want to minimize how often
        they think about their roof, or who specifically want the look of a
        standing-seam or panel system.
      - >-
        It's less likely to make sense as a like-for-like swap when budget
        is the primary constraint, since the upfront cost is meaningfully
        higher than an asphalt shingle replacement. An inspection and a
        straight conversation about your goals is the best way to figure
        out which direction fits your situation.

  - heading: What A Professional Installation Should Address
    intro: >-
      The quality of a metal roof depends heavily on the installation
      itself, not just the material. A professional installation should
      account for:
    items:
      - title: Roof deck condition
        description: The deck underneath needs to be sound before new roofing goes on top of it.
      - title: Flashing and details
        description: Flashing at valleys, edges, and transitions is where most roof leaks originate if it's done poorly.
      - title: Penetrations
        description: Vents, chimneys, and other penetrations need panel-specific flashing to stay watertight.
      - title: Water management
        description: Proper overlap, fastening, and drainage paths keep water moving off the roof instead of under it.
      - title: Ventilation
        description: Attic ventilation affects how the roofing system performs over time and shouldn't be an afterthought.
      - title: Manufacturer installation requirements
        description: Metal roofing manufacturers specify exact installation methods. Following them is what keeps any manufacturer warranty valid.
