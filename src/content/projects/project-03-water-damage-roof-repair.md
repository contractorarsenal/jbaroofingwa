educationSections:
  - heading: How Water Gets Into A Roof
    intro: >-
      Water intrusion usually comes from one of a handful of places, not a
      mystery leak that shows up out of nowhere.
    items:
      - title: Damaged shingles
        description: Cracked, curling, or missing shingles leave the decking underneath exposed to weather.
      - title: Failed flashing
        description: Flashing around chimneys, vents, and valleys is a common failure point when it's damaged or was installed incorrectly.
      - title: Roof penetrations
        description: Any vent pipe, skylight, or roof-mounted fixture is a potential entry point if it isn't properly sealed.
      - title: Drainage and water-management issues
        description: Water needs a clear path off the roof. When drainage details are positioned incorrectly, water can pool or get directed under roofing materials instead of away from them.
      - title: Aging materials
        description: Roofing materials lose their protective qualities over time, even without any single obvious point of failure.

  - heading: Why Fixing The Source Matters
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Covering water damage without addressing what let the water in
        doesn't solve the problem. It just delays it, and often makes the
        eventual repair bigger and more expensive once the damage has had
        more time to spread.
      - >-
        That's why an inspection focuses on finding where water is actually
        getting in, not just patching the spot where it happened to show up
        inside the house.

watchFor:
  - Water staining on ceilings or walls
  - Soft or spongy decking when walked on
  - Repeated leaks in the same area
  - Visible moss or heavy aging on the roof surface
  - Damaged flashing or other roof details
