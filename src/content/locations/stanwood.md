**Begin with the diagnosis.** Before you look at any number, find the sentence that says what is wrong with the roof. One damaged area on a roof with plenty of life left is usually a repair. Widespread wear, repeated repairs in the same spots, or a roof near the end of its expected lifespan usually points toward replacement. If a proposal jumps to the bigger job without saying why, ask. After an inspection, JBA's answer is meant to be a straight one, not a default toward replacement.

**Then read the scope.** A useful estimate says what is coming off and what is going on: the material and its quality, the tear-off, and what is included, such as cleanup and disposal. It should also give a realistic timeline. Two proposals with different totals are often describing different amounts of work, so the scope is what makes them comparable.

**Find the decking clause.** New roofing sits directly on the deck, so damaged or rotted decking has to be replaced first. Its condition often can't be fully confirmed until the old roof is removed. A good proposal says how that situation is handled. JBA shows you what it finds before proceeding, rather than deciding for you or adding a surprise line to the final invoice.

**Treat material as a decision.** Metal generally offers a longer service-life potential and lower routine maintenance, but costs more upfront and needs a specific installation technique. Asphalt shingles cost less upfront, are widely understood by roofing crews, and are usually simpler to repair individually. Switching an existing shingle roof to metal is a full tear-off and replacement, not an overlay, because a metal system has different installation requirements. Metal isn't right for every home, and JBA will tell you whether it fits your goals rather than defaulting to it. The photos below follow one metal replacement from the existing roof to the finished result.

**Ask about permits.** Sometimes a permit applies, depending on the property and the scope of the project. JBA reviews what's required for your specific job as part of the estimate and handles the paperwork if a permit applies, so ask where that appears in the proposal.
