**If water is coming in.** Treat it as urgent. Call JBA or start a repair request right away so someone can get eyes on the roof. If it's a stain that isn't growing, it can typically wait for a scheduled inspection, though it's still worth having looked at soon.

**What usually causes a leak.** Most leaks come from failed flashing around a chimney or vent, a roof penetration that was never properly sealed, missing or damaged shingles, poor drainage, or storm damage. Moss and debris holding water against the roof deck longer than it was built to handle is another common factor in Western Washington.

**After a storm.** Wind, hail, and falling debris can lift or crack shingles, damage flashing, or expose the roof deck. JBA inspects the full roof after storm damage, not just the obviously affected area, since wind can lift shingles in spots that aren't visible from the ground.

**Repair, or something bigger?** Missing or damaged shingles in a specific area can typically be replaced without touching the rest of the roof, as long as matching material is available and the surrounding roofing is still in good condition. Widespread missing shingles across the roof point toward a bigger conversation, and you get a straight answer after the roof is inspected.

**What a repair can involve.** The photos below follow one water-damage repair. An improperly positioned drainage detail had been letting water into the roofing system, and the water had reached the decking. The damaged decking was removed rather than covered over, and new shingles went on sound decking. Fixing the drainage detail was part of the repair, because replacing shingles without addressing the source would have let the same problem happen again.
