**Start with what you're seeing.** Water coming through a ceiling or wall is a repair request, and an urgent one if it is active. A stain that isn't growing, or a roof whose condition you simply don't know, calls for an inspection first. Widespread wear, repeated repairs in the same spots, or a roof near the end of its expected life is the point to ask for a replacement estimate.

**How JBA decides between repair and replacement.** After inspecting the roof, the question is how far the damage extends and how much life the roof has left. A single damaged area on a roof with plenty of life left is usually a repair. Widespread wear usually points toward replacement. You get a straight answer, not a default toward the bigger job, and a written summary with photos that you can keep or share.

**What to ask about decking before you accept an estimate.** New roofing sits directly on the deck, so damaged or rotted decking has to be replaced first. Decking condition often can't be confirmed until the old roof is removed. Ask how any decking damage would be handled and shown to you, and whether the estimate spells that out. JBA shows you what it finds before proceeding.

**Weather and the schedule.** Weather can move a start date or pause a job, and shingle manufacturers set installation requirements of their own. A good estimate says how both affect the timeline. If it doesn't, ask.
