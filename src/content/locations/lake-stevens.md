**Before the assessment.** Jot down where you've seen the problem and when it started, and find out roughly how old the roof is. The online assessment request asks for the property's ZIP, what you need help with, what you're noticing, the roof's approximate age, how soon you need help, and whether an insurance claim is involved. A photo is optional but useful. Once you submit it, JBA schedules a time to look at the roof and gives you a written estimate.

**Comparing roofing estimates.** Put the estimates side by side and compare the same items in each: the material and quality being installed, the tear-off scope, how decking damage would be handled, a realistic timeline, and what is included, such as cleanup and disposal. A quote that is vague on any of those is worth a question before you sign.

**If the decking is damaged.** New roofing sits directly on the deck, so rotted or damaged decking has to be replaced first. Whether it is damaged often can't be confirmed until the old roof comes off, so ask each contractor what happens if it turns up: how you will be told, and how the work will be written down. JBA shows you what it finds before proceeding. The photos below show what an exposed deck looks like.

**Weather and scheduling.** Asphalt shingles are normally installed on dry decking, so a rainy stretch can delay a start or pause a job. Ask each contractor how rain days are handled and how schedule changes are communicated.
