**When a repair may be enough.** A single damaged area on a roof with a lot of life left is usually a repair. Widespread wear, repeated repairs in the same spots, or a roof already near the end of its expected lifespan usually points toward replacement. The inspection is what separates the two.

**If damaged decking is found.** Damaged or rotted decking has to be replaced before new roofing goes down, because the shingles sit directly on the deck. Decking condition often can't be fully confirmed until the old roof is removed, so JBA shows you what was found before the work continues.

**What a replacement quote should cover.** A useful estimate spells out the material and quality being installed, the tear-off scope, how decking damage would be handled if it turns up, a realistic timeline, and what is included, such as cleanup and disposal. If a quote is vague on any of those, ask before you sign.

**How weather affects scheduling.** Weather can change when exterior roofing work can happen, and shingle manufacturers set installation requirements of their own. Before you sign, ask how weather and those requirements affect the proposed schedule, and what happens to the timeline if conditions change.

**Leaks.** If water is actively coming into the house, treat it as urgent and request service right away. A stain that isn't growing can usually wait for a scheduled inspection, though it is still worth having looked at soon.

For a repair, start with [roof repair in Everett](/services/roof-repair/). If you're weighing a new roof, start with [roof replacement](/services/roof-replacement/). If you just want a clear picture of the roof's condition, a [roof inspection](/services/roof-inspection/) is the right first step.
