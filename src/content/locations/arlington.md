**Start with the inspection.** An inspection documents the roof's condition, including anything that points toward a repair, a replacement, or nothing at all. It isn't a sales call: if everything checks out, that's what JBA will tell you. Afterward, JBA walks you through what it found in plain language, discusses practical next steps, and gives you a written summary with photos that you can keep or share.

**How the findings guide the next step.** Localized damage points toward a repair. Moss and light buildup usually point toward cleaning. Widespread wear, or a roof well past its expected lifespan, points toward replacement. What JBA recommends depends on what the inspection actually turns up.

**When to request an assessment.** Common times are before buying or selling a home, after a major storm, before winter, or any time it has been several years since the roof was last looked at. To start, [request an assessment](/assessment/). It asks what you're noticing, how old the roof is, and how soon you need help.

**Questions to ask about recommended work.** Whatever the recommendation, ask to see what was found and where, and why it calls for a repair rather than a replacement, or the reverse. For a replacement, ask what the estimate includes: the material and quality, the tear-off scope, how decking damage would be handled, a realistic timeline, and cleanup and disposal.

**Tree cover and debris.** Properties with overhanging trees tend to collect debris and moss faster, which is why some Snohomish County homeowners schedule [cleaning](/services/roof-cleaning/) or [maintenance](/services/roof-maintenance/) more than once a year. JBA can give a specific recommendation once it has seen the property.

**Permits.** Whether a permit applies depends on the property, the scope of work, and who has jurisdiction over the address. The City of Arlington lists a [Re-Roof Application](https://www.arlingtonwa.gov/246/Development-Permit-Applications) on its permit page, says a permit is required before constructing or installing equipment under most circumstances, and asks that you check with its Building Division before any work is done. Snohomish County's [residential permit page](https://snohomishcountywa.gov/2908/Residential-Building-Permits) lists re-roofing among its permit examples. JBA reviews what's required for your job as part of the estimate and handles the paperwork if a permit applies.
