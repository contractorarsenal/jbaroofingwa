problemHeading: Why Does Moss Grow On Washington Roofs?
problemStatement: >-
  Moss needs shade and consistent moisture to establish itself, and Western
  Washington roofs get plenty of both. North-facing slopes, roofs under
  heavy tree cover, and areas that stay damp between rain events are the
  most common places it takes hold, especially on asphalt shingles, which
  give it a rougher surface to grip than metal or tile.

  - eyebrow: The Risk
    heading: Can Moss Damage A Roof?
    tone: dark
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Yes, over time. Moss doesn't just sit on top of shingles. As it
        grows, it can lift shingle edges and work its way underneath them,
        which gives water a path it wouldn't otherwise have. Left long
        enough, that moisture held against the roof deck can lead to the
        kind of decking damage that turns a cleaning job into a repair.
      - >-
        Visible moss, black streaking, or a roof that hasn't been cleaned in
        several years are the usual signs it's time to look at cleaning.
        Heavier tree cover generally means more frequent attention.

  - eyebrow: A Common Question
    heading: Why Shouldn't I Just Pressure Wash My Roof?
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        High-pressure cleaning can strip the protective granules off asphalt
        shingles and damage the surface underneath, which is why we don't
        use it. Cleaning methods should match the roofing material and the
        manufacturer's guidance, not whatever equipment happens to be on
        the truck.
      - >-
        We use low-pressure treatment methods on asphalt shingle roofs
        instead, which kill moss and organic growth without stripping the
        surface.

  - eyebrow: Know The Limits
    heading: When Cleaning Isn't Enough
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        If moss has already caused shingle damage or water has reached the
        decking, cleaning alone won't fix that. At that point, a repair (or
        in some cases replacement) addresses the underlying damage, with
        cleaning as part of getting the roof back to a maintainable
        condition.
      - >-
        Between cleanings, keeping gutters clear and trimming back
        overhanging branches where practical both help slow how quickly
        moss and debris build back up.
